2026-11-07 ALMO-EDA in Psi4 (design)#

Status: design, not started. Option C of the ALMO-EDA work (Paul’s route, 2026-11-06: skip a PySCF CPU port, build the Interaction EDA plug-in on GPU4PySCF, then add Psi4 as its CPU backend). Estimate 3–5 weeks for the Python-only scope below, plus 2–4 weeks if exact ωB97M-V parity is wanted, which needs C++ in Psi4.

Goal#

Second-generation ALMO energy decomposition analysis (ALMO-EDA2) of intermolecular interactions on CPUs, with an unmodified Psi4 1.11, giving the same terms as the GPU4PySCF backend of the Interaction EDA step (interaction_eda_step):

  • frozen, split into electrostatic (orthogonal decomposition), classical electrostatic, Pauli and dispersion;

  • polarization, by fragment electric-field response functions (FERF);

  • charge transfer, which carries the basis-set superposition error;

  • the total, which must equal the supermolecular DFT interaction energy.

Where it runs: the Interaction EDA step gains a Psi4 backend. Like the GPU4PySCF one, it writes input.json and a driver script into the step’s directory and runs the driver with the Python of the Psi4 environment (psi4.ini). The driver uses Psi4 as a library (import psi4); it is not a Psi4 input file. This page lives in the Psi4 step’s campaigns because the Psi4-side work is the substance; the step itself (psi4_step) does not change.

The reference is the GPU4PySCF implementation the Interaction EDA step already uses, validated to 1.4 × 10⁻⁶ kJ/mol against science’s standalone runs (see interaction_eda_step’s docs). Its definitions are the specification: where this page and GPU4PySCF’s eda.py differ, eda.py wins.

Python only, or C++?#

Psi4 has no ALMO-EDA, no SCF-MI and no FERF. But the expensive pieces are C++ objects that Psi4 already exports to Python, and ALMO-EDA2 needs nothing else from C++, with one exception: the exchange-correlation response kernel.

Needed

Psi4 1.11, from Python (checked in the v1.11 source)

Coulomb and exchange, incl. range-separated, for any density

core.JK.build(basis), jk.C_left_add/C_right_add, set_do_wK/set_omega, compute, J()/K()/wK()

XC potential and energy, incl. VV10

core.VBase.build(basis, functional, "RV"), set_D, compute_V, quadrature_values

XC response kernel (coupled response)

VBase.compute_Vx – LDA and GGA only: RV::compute_Vx_full throws for VV10 and for meta-GGAs

Functionals, VV10 on/off, ω

build_superfunctional, set_do_vv10, x_omega, is_meta, needs_vv10

Fragment SCF and its CPKS solver

energy(..., return_wfn=True); wfn.cphf_solve, cphf_Hx, onel_Hx/twoel_Hx (these use Vx, so the same limit applies)

Overlap, dipole and quadrupole integrals

MintsHelper.ao_overlap, ao_dipole, ao_quadrupole

D4, if a -D4 functional is used

Psi4’s empirical dispersion (dftd4)

So the scope splits by functional:

  • ωB97X-V (a range-separated hybrid GGA plus VV10): VV10 in the SCF and SCF-MI, coupled FERF with the GGA kernel and without the VV10 kernel. The VV10 response term is negligible: dropping it moved energy between polarization and charge transfer by at most 0.0014 kJ/mol for a Li⁺ shell, and does not change the total (GPU4PySCF, mlff’s xc_cmp-15003). All Python.

  • ωB97M-V with uncoupled FERF (orbital-energy denominators, no kernel): all Python. Uncoupled FERF moves about 1 % of the polarization into charge transfer (shell A: +1.49 kJ/mol); frozen and total are unchanged.

  • ωB97M-V with coupled FERF, exactly as GPU4PySCF: needs a meta-GGA Vx kernel in Psi4’s C++ (RV::compute_Vx_full, “does not support rotated V builds for MGGA’s”). 2–4 weeks, best done with the Psi4 developers; who contacts them is Paul’s decision.

The method#

The fragments’ orbitals are expanded only in their own atoms’ basis functions (absolutely localized MOs, ALMOs), so the coefficient matrix is block-diagonal and the MOs of different fragments are non-orthogonal. Every energy below is the full system’s DFT energy functional, evaluated at the density

\[P = C\,(C^{\mathsf T} S\, C)^{-1} C^{\mathsf T},\]

built from the block-diagonal \(C\); \(S\) is the full AO overlap. That one formula, with Psi4’s JK and VBase for the two-electron and XC parts, is the whole engine.

  1. Isolated fragments. An SCF of each fragment in its own basis (monomer basis, not the dimer basis: the BSSE goes into charge transfer, as in Q-Chem and GPU4PySCF). Energies \(E_A\), occupied orbitals \(C_A\).

  2. Frozen state. \(C_{\rm frz} = \mathrm{diag}(C_A, C_B, \dots)\); the frozen interaction is \(E[P_{\rm frz}] - \sum_A E_A\).

  3. Frozen decomposition (Horn, Mao and Head-Gordon 2016):

    • classical electrostatic: the Coulomb interaction of the isolated fragments’ charge densities (electrons and nuclei), from J of each fragment density;

    • electrostatic (orthogonal decomposition): from the fragment densities projected as in the paper;

    • dispersion: the frozen energy with the functional minus that with its dispersion-free counterpart, exactly as eda.py defines it for each functional (milestone 0 transcribes the definition);

    • Pauli = frozen − electrostatic − dispersion; the common (SAPT-comparable) Pauli = frozen − classical electrostatic − dispersion.

  4. FERF polarization space. For each fragment, the response of its occupied orbitals to uniform fields (dipole) and field gradients (quadrupole), i.e. 3 + 6 CPKS solves per fragment with the dipole and quadrupole integrals as perturbations:

    • coupled: wfn.cphf_solve on the fragment’s wavefunction, whose Hessian uses Vx (GGA; VV10 switched off for the response only);

    • uncoupled: orbital-energy denominators, no kernel.

    The responses are orthogonalized per fragment and truncated by singular value (threshold 10⁻⁴, the validated default; the step warns above it for cations). Together with the occupied orbitals they span each fragment’s polarization space.

  5. SCF-MI in the FERF space. Minimize \(E[P(C)]\) over block-diagonal \(C\) whose fragment blocks rotate only within their polarization spaces. Gradient: the full Fock matrix \(F[P]\) from JK + VBase, projected onto each fragment’s occupied-virtual rotations in the non-orthogonal metric (the locally projected equations of Stoll and of Khaliullin et al.). Solver: DIIS or a quasi-Newton step in the rotation parameters; the space is small (9 × nocc per fragment), so this is cheap besides the Fock builds. Polarization = \(E_{\rm pol} - E_{\rm frz}\).

  6. Full SCF of the whole system in the full basis (Psi4’s own energy(), started from the SCF-MI density). Charge transfer = \(E_{\rm full} - E_{\rm pol}\). Total = \(E_{\rm full} - \sum_A E_A\).

Each Fock build is one JK + VBase call on the whole system, so an SCF-MI iteration costs about one SCF iteration. Expected cost: the fragment SCFs, one full SCF and an SCF-MI of comparable length, plus 9 CPKS solves per fragment. Psi4 runs the SCF about 1.7× faster than CPU PySCF (Owl, 32 cores: shell A 489 s vs 826 s).

Checks built into the driver#

  • frozen + polarization + charge transfer = total, to rounding;

  • the total equals Psi4’s supermolecular interaction energy (full SCF minus the fragment SCFs) – an independent check of the energy functional at P;

  • frozen = electrostatic + Pauli + dispersion = classical electrostatic + common Pauli + dispersion;

  • SCF-MI converged in its space, and its energy between frozen and full.

Validation against GPU4PySCF#

The same structures and settings as the GPU4PySCF validation (interaction_eda_step tests and science’s references):

  • S22 water dimer; Li⁺–EC at R = 1.20, 1.50, 1.70 Å; shell A (Li⁺, 2 EC, 2 DMC) pairs and cluster; Li⁺–PF₆⁻ and PF₆⁻–EC (anions);

  • ωB97X-V/def2-TZVPD, coupled FERF without the VV10 kernel, against GPU4PySCF with nlc in the response off;

  • ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD with uncoupled FERF, against GPU4PySCF uncoupled;

  • exact integrals in both (no density fitting), the same grids where the codes allow.

Gates: every term within 0.1 kJ/mol, aiming for 0.01; the total within 0.01 kJ/mol of each code’s own supermolecular interaction energy. Differences beyond that are expected only from the DFT grids, and are measured first on the water dimer by comparing plain DFT interaction energies.

Risks#

  • The orthogonal electrostatic decomposition has details (which projector, which densities) that must match eda.py exactly; milestone 0 exists for this.

  • SCF-MI convergence for strongly bound ions (Li⁺) in the non-orthogonal metric; fall back to a trust-region step if DIIS struggles.

  • Grids: Psi4 and GPU4PySCF build different DFT grids, so their terms can differ at the 0.01–0.1 kJ/mol level. If that exceeds the gate, tighten both grids rather than chase it in the EDA.

  • Python-side cost for big clusters: the dense algebra in the metric is \(O(N^3)\) with small prefactors; the Fock builds dominate. Measured in milestone 7.

  • Psi4 internals: the driver uses exported but lightly documented objects (VBase, cphf_solve); pin the Psi4 version the backend supports.

Decisions for Paul#

  • Which functional the CPU backend targets first: ωB97X-V (coupled, Python only) or ωB97M-V (uncoupled, Python only, about 1 % of polarization moved to charge transfer).

  • Whether to do milestone 8 (C++ meta-GGA kernel), and who contacts the Psi4 developers about it – and about contributing the ALMO-EDA driver to Psi4 itself.
